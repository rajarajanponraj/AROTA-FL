TITLE:
Convergence-Aware Active RIS for AirComp-Based Federated Learning Under Imperfect CSI

ABSTRACT:
Over-the-Air Federated Learning (AirFL) enables distributed edge devices to aggregate local model parameters concurrently over shared radio spectrum, decoupling transmission latency from the user population. However, the analog superposition principle is vulnerable to channel fading, active reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) amplification noise, and channel state information (CSI) estimation errors. Conventional AirFL designs commonly optimize transceiver scalers and surface reflection coefficients by minimizing instantaneous signal mean-squared error (MSE) evaluated on estimated channels. This work reveals that under imperfect CSI, such naive MSE minimization aggressively saturates active RIS amplification, severely inflating aggregated channel uncertainties and stalling federated learning convergence. To resolve this breakdown, we formulate a convergence-aware joint transceiver and active RIS optimization framework. By taking the conditional expectation of the physical aggregation error over the distribution of CSI estimation uncertainty, we analytically derive an exact regularized penalty that couples surface amplification gain with transmit power and error variance. We establish a direct analytical linkage between the derived objective and the theoretical descent guarantee of L-smooth federated optimization. Exploiting closed-form zero-forcing transceiver scaling, the joint problem is reduced to bound-constrained optimization over N+1 variables, exhibiting a lightweight per-iteration complexity of O(N^2) that is substantially lower than semidefinite relaxation methods (O(N^4.5)). Extensive physical-layer sweeps and PyTorch simulations on MNIST and CIFAR-10 demonstrate that the proposed controller dynamically clamps active amplification, achieving up to 10x lower aggregation MSE under high CSI uncertainty, 3x faster learning convergence, and test accuracies that closely trace ideal noise-free communications.

Active RIS, Over-the-Air Computation, Federated Learning, Imperfect CSI, Convergence Analysis, Edge Intelligence.

=== SECTION: Introduction ===

The rapid deployment of intelligent edge devices has driven the shift toward decentralized machine learning paradigms. Federated Learning (FL) enables distributed clients to train a shared global model iteratively while maintaining raw training samples on edge devices . In conventional digital communications based on orthogonal multiple access (OMA), participating clients sequentially upload high-dimensional parameter updates or gradients to a central parameter server. As the client population K or parameter dimension d grows, this orthogonal transmission pipeline incurs severe uplink latency and spectrum congestion, creating an operational bottleneck at the wireless edge .

To overcome bandwidth limitations, Over-the-Air Computation (AirComp) has emerged as an analog physical-layer aggregation mechanism . Exploiting the natural superposition property of electromagnetic waves in a multiple-access channel, AirComp coordinates synchronized analog transmissions across edge devices. The central Access Point (AP) receives the sum of simultaneous analog waveforms directly in a single channel use, reducing aggregation latency to a constant independent of the client count K . 

Nevertheless, the fidelity of analog AirComp depends critically on accurate phase alignment and amplitude normalization. When wireless channels undergo severe multipath fading or non-line-of-sight (NLoS) blockage, signal attenuation degrades the received signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), causing aggregation errors that slow down or destabilize federated optimization . Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RISs) have recently been introduced to establish favorable virtual line-of-sight (LoS) links . However, traditional passive RISs rely exclusively on passive phase-shifting elements. Consequently, passive RIS-assisted links suffer from the fundamental "double-fading" (multiplicative path-loss) attenuation, requiring massive element counts N to achieve meaningful signal enhancements .

Active RIS technology addresses this limitation by integrating active reflection-type amplifiers into the surface elements . Active RISs can simultaneously adjust signal phases and amplify reflected amplitudes, effectively compensating for multiplicative propagation losses with compact physical apertures . Despite these structural gains, active RIS deployments introduce two coupled challenges:

• Thermal Noise Amplification: The active components introduce inherent thermal noise at the surface, which is amplified and forwarded to the AP receiver .
 
• CSI Uncertainty Inflation: Obtaining perfect Channel State Information (CSI) for high-dimensional reflected channels is challenging in practice . In existing literature, beamforming and surface configurations are commonly designed by minimizing the estimated instantaneous aggregation mean-squared error (MSE), treating imperfect channel estimates as ground truth .

In this paper, we demonstrate that naive instantaneous MSE minimization breaks down when applied to active RIS-assisted AirFL under imperfect CSI. Because estimated channels omit the stochastic variance of estimation errors, the solver drives the active amplification gain to its hardware ceiling. This excessive amplification heavily inflates both the active thermal noise and the underlying channel estimation errors, injecting severe noise into the global gradient update and degrading FL convergence.

--- Contributions ---

To address this vulnerability, we propose a convergence-aware optimization framework for active-RIS-assisted AirComp federated learning. Our key contributions are:

• Closed-Form Expected Error Formulation: We derive the exact conditional expectation of aggregation error over Gaussian CSI uncertainties, exposing an analytical regularization penalty proportional to amplification gain, transmit power, and CSI error variance.
 
• FL Convergence Linkage: Using the L-smooth descent lemma, we prove that the derived objective directly minimizes the upper bound on expected per-round loss reduction.
 
• Low-Complexity Algorithm: We establish closed-form ZF transceiver scaling, reducing the joint problem to N+1 active RIS variables solved via Quasi-Newton optimization in O(N^2) complexity, bypassing expensive SDR solvers.
 
• Cross-Layer Validation: Extensive physical-layer sweeps (500 Monte Carlo seeds) and PyTorch FL simulations on MNIST/CIFAR-10 under non-IID partitions demonstrate up to 10x MSE reduction and 3x faster convergence over naive baselines.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II details the system model; Section III develops the convergence-aware formulation; Section IV presents the joint algorithm; Section V reports simulation results; and Section VI concludes the paper.

Per objective evaluation, computing G^H Theta h_k requires O(K N) operations, \|G^H Theta\|^2 requires O(N), and transceiver factors c^*, \b_k^*\ require O(K), giving O(K N) total cost per function call. With D = N+1 optimization variables, the per-iteration Quasi-Newton update scales as O(D^2) = O(N^2). Thus, for I_iter iterations and M restarts, the overall complexity is O(M * I_iter * (K N + N^2)) O(N^2) for N > K. In contrast, Semidefinite Relaxation (SDR) requires O(N^4.5) , demonstrating that our framework is well-suited for real-time edge execution.

=== SECTION: Conclusion ===

This work examined active-RIS-assisted Over-the-Air Federated Learning under imperfect CSI. We identified a fundamental vulnerability in conventional designs: naive instantaneous MSE minimization drives active amplification to its ceiling, amplifying thermal noise and compounding CSI estimation errors. To solve this, we formulated a convergence-aware framework derived from the conditional expectation of aggregation error, providing an exact regularization penalty that dynamically throttles amplification under uncertainty. Combining closed-form zero-forcing transceiver scaling with bound-constrained Quasi-Newton optimization delivers an efficient O(N^2) algorithmic solution. Physical-layer sweeps and PyTorch simulations on MNIST and CIFAR-10 confirmed that our framework prevents noise divergence, reduces aggregation MSE by up to 10x, and accelerates learning convergence across non-IID edge networks. Future work will investigate multi-antenna transceivers and discrete phase quantization.
